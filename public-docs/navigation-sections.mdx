Tabs, groups, anchors, and links, how each renders on the site, and how to control what's published, hidden, or private.

The **Docs** view is a tree of sections and pages.
Drag to reorder or nest, and the site follows.
Every section has a kind that decides how it renders.

## The four kinds

| Kind | On the site | Can hold pages | Can have a URL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Group** | A labeled, collapsible list of pages in the sidebar | Yes | No |
| **Tab** | A section in the site header. The sidebar shows only that tab's pages. | Yes | Optional |
| **Anchor** | A heading in the sidebar that never collapses, with its pages under it | Yes | Optional |
| **Link** | An entry in the navigation that goes to another website | No | Required |

![A published site with header tabs, a sidebar anchor, and groups](/images/public-docs-navigation-site.png)

### Groups

A group is what **Add folder** creates.
On the site it's a collapsible section that starts open.
Visitors can collapse it, and the site remembers their choice; a group that holds the current page always opens.
Groups nest inside groups, anchors, and tabs.

### Tabs

Tabs split a large site into top-level areas, such as Documentation and API reference.
They sit directly under the site root; a tab nested deeper renders as a group.
The header shows one entry per tab, and clicking one lands on its first page.
On phones the tabs appear at the top of the sidebar.

Give a tab a URL and it becomes an **external link tab**: it goes to another site and holds no pages.
A tab with no pages and no URL isn't shown as a tab.

Pages outside every tab still publish.
They show in the sidebar when the visitor is on one of them; on a site where everything else is in tabs, a visitor on such a page sees the first tab's sidebar.

### Anchors

An anchor is a heading in the sidebar.
Unlike a group it never folds, so its pages are always visible.
Use anchors to break a long sidebar into named parts.
An anchor with a URL and no pages renders as a plain link.

### Links

A link points at another website, such as a community forum or your main site.
It can't hold pages, and its address has to start with `http://` or `https://`.

## Create and change sections

Right-click the **Published** header or a section, or click its **...** button.

| To | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Add a group | **Add folder** |
| Add a tab | **Add tab** on the Published header |
| Add an anchor | **Add anchor** |
| Add an external link in the header | **Other > Add external link tab**, then enter a **Label** and a **Destination** URL |
| Change a section's kind | **Convert to**, then **Group**, **Tab**, or **Anchor**. **Tab** is offered only for sections directly under the site root. |
| Change a link's address | Click the section; its link dialog opens |
| Reorder or nest | Drag, or use **Move** |
| Rename | **Rename**, or click the name |
| Remove | **Remove from Docs**. Pages keep their files. See [Unlink a page](/public-docs/docs-and-files-views#unlink-a-page). |

Creating and converting sections needs an organization owner.
A section with no pages is left out of the site.

## Private and hidden

Right-click a section and open **Publishing options**.
The two settings are independent.

| Option | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Restrict access** | Marks the section private. Nothing in it is built, so it's absent from the site, search, Ask the docs, the sitemap, and `llms.txt`. Choose **Open access** to publish it again. |
| **Hide from navigation** | Builds the pages but leaves them out of the sidebar and search, and marks them `noindex` for search engines. Visitors can still open them from a direct link. Choose **Show in navigation** to list them again. |

Access is inherited.
A section with no setting of its own follows its parent, and the site root is public, so the menu always shows the opposite of the section's current effective state.
A private section's pages are private too, and the setting is stored only where it differs from the parent.

In the Docs tree, a section with its own setting shows a small icon after its name: a lock for **Private: not built on the site**, or a crossed-out eye for **Hidden: left out of the site menu and search**.
Pages inside it inherit without showing the icon.

To publish one page inside a private section, set `access: public` in that page's frontmatter.

## Page settings

Frontmatter on a page controls how it appears in the navigation and on the site.

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `title` | The page title. By default it's the page's first heading. |
| `sidebarTitle` | A shorter label for the sidebar |
| `description` | The summary shown in search results, link previews, and `llms.txt` |
| `tableOfContents` | `false` hides the on-page table of contents |
| `hidden` | `true` is the same as **Hide from navigation** for one page. Its children move up into its place. |
| `access` | `public` or `private`, the same as **Publishing options** for one page. Wins over the section's setting. |
| `noindex` | `true` keeps the page out of search engines, the sitemap, and the llms files. The page still serves. |
| `pagefind` | `false` leaves the page out of site search and Ask the docs |

A page's address comes from its file path, not from its frontmatter or its place in Docs.
See [Docs view and Files view](/public-docs/docs-and-files-views).
